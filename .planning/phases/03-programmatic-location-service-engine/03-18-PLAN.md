The `geo.js` boundary — the single most dangerous line in the phase (Pitfall 5). Use `forGeo` from
`towns.js`; do not re-derive it:

```js
// geo.js:46,56 build `/location/${town.region}/…`. towns.js calls that field `urlRegion`, and
// TownCard.region is the DISPLAY county. Opposite meanings, same name. A raw row passed in
// yields `/location/undefined/...` on every link of every generated page.
nearestTowns(forGeo(town), PUBLISHED_TOWNS.map(forGeo), n);
```

The nearby `meta` is the destination town's **county**, not the distance (§13-G). Pass the optional
`metaFor` callback plan 03-02 added to `buildInterlinks`. `46.2 miles away` on a Telford page
advertises the opposite of local, and Success Criterion 4 requires geography in the **ordering**, not
in a published distance matrix.

Import geography from `@bhc/design-system/geo`, never the barrel — the barrel re-exports through JSX
and a plain `node` import of it fails, which would make every acceptance check in this plan
unrunnable.

`TownCard` contract, §7.1: `town` is the display name, `region` is the **display county** (Banbury
renders `Oxfordshire · 6 services` while its combo URLs live under `/location/warwickshire/`),
`serviceCount` is `6`, `headingLevel` is `3` because the county name is the `<h2>`.

The four new `<h2>`s this plan authors, from §5's deck: `Towns near {Town}` (hub),
`Towns we cover for {service noun}` (service page), `Areas we cover` (move-in only),
`Towns we cover` (home teaser). The two `geo.js` defaults — `Other cleaning services in {Town}` and
`{Service} in nearby towns` — are unchanged and must not be overridden.
