**These are eight different SUBJECTS, not eight rewordings of one.** A task worded "write eight
variations of the domestic-cleaning copy" has already failed: delta 23's Jaccard is lexical, eight
paraphrases share almost no 5-grams and measure ≈0.05, and the gate would report green on exactly
the problem it exists to detect. The eight subjects for this service, one per entry, in this order —
index 0 through 7 is the `a` value a town carries, so the order is data, not presentation:

  0. The kitchen on a regular visit — what gets done every time and what gets done in rotation.
  1. Bathrooms — limescale, the things that go wrong between visits, why the same cleaner matters.
  2. Floors and the hoovering that people think is the whole job — carpet vs hard floor, edges,
     under furniture, what a vacuum genuinely cannot reach.
  3. Dust, and where it actually lives — tops of frames, skirtings, blinds, radiators, the rotation
     that keeps it from building.
  4. Beds, laundry and the surfaces around them — what we will and will not do with someone's
     bedroom.
  5. Living with pets and children — the cleaning that is different in a house that is genuinely
     used, and where the honest limits are.
  6. Weekly versus fortnightly versus monthly — what changes about the job at each interval, and
     which one a house actually needs.
  7. What a regular clean deliberately is NOT — the jobs that belong to a deep clean or a builders
     clean, and why folding them in makes both worse.

Hard rules, each with a gate behind it:
  - **No town name and no county name anywhere in this file, including comments.** The town appears
    in the `<h1>`, the hero lead, the locality paragraph and the interlink headings — places where a
    proper noun belongs. §10.2's first prohibition: no sentence may be produced by substituting a
    town name into an otherwise fixed sentence.
  - **No postcode, no postcode district, no street line, no phone number, no email, no price.**
    D-04 / Lock 5, and the site quotes rather than price-lists. Do not name the banned shapes in a
    comment either; this repo has been bitten twelve times by a comment that instanced the string
    its own gate greps for.
  - No `h2` may begin `Domestic Cleaning in ` — delta 20 asserts the `{noun} in {Town}` phrase
    appears in exactly one heading per page, and that heading is the `<h1>`.
  - Plain UK English: *hoover*, *skirting boards*, *tenancy*, *flat*. Contractions. Concrete nouns.
    Never *your local area*.
  - A literal `&` where an ampersand is wanted, never the entity — the lock's extractor decodes
    before comparing, so the entity double-encodes.
